# Research methodology and proposed validation

## Literature assessment
A targeted qualitative synthesis of primary research addressing (1) BUB1B-associated MVA and BUBR1 checkpoint dysfunction, (2) rescue with functional BUBR1, and (3) NAD⁺–SIRT2–BUBR1 regulation and NMN. Claims were categorized as directly demonstrated, mechanistically inferred, or untested. This was not a systematic review or meta-analysis.

## Proposed study — not performed
Use independently characterized BUB1B-related MVA cellular models with documented residual functional BUBR1. Include healthy reference cells.

| Arm | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Healthy reference | Normal baseline |
| MVA + vehicle | Disease baseline |
| MVA + NMN | Candidate effect |
| MVA + functional BUBR1 restoration | Positive functional benchmark |
| MVA + NMN + functional BUBR1 restoration | Explore complementary effects |
| MVA + NMN with SIRT2 function disrupted | Assess SIRT2 dependence |

**Primary endpoint:** frequency of chromosome segregation errors compared with MVA vehicle.

**Secondary endpoints:** full-length BUBR1 abundance, spindle assembly checkpoint performance, BUBR1 acetylation where feasible, SIRT2 dependence, viability, and mitotic progression.

**Advancement criteria (proposed, not validated cutoffs):**
1. Reproducible reduction in segregation errors relative to vehicle.
2. Consistent checkpoint improvement, not merely increased protein abundance.
3. Mechanistic evidence consistent with BUBR1 stabilization and SIRT2 involvement.
4. Independent biological replication and appropriate statistical analysis.
5. No material deterioration in cell health or mitotic progression.

**Interpretation:** A rise in BUBR1 protein without improved segregation is target engagement only. Effects independent of SIRT2 require alternative mechanistic explanations. Absence of functional BUBR1 may prevent a stabilization-only strategy from succeeding.

No patient-specific genomic records, raw sequencing data, or patient identifiers are needed for this proposal.
